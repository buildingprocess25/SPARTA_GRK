## Finding 1 — Inverted muted-text color pairing fails contrast, used 162 times across 31 files (HIGH)

**Rule:** R-25 (Color Contrast) — Hard Gate. WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text/non-text UI.

**Where:** the app has two different "muted/secondary text" color pairings in active use:
- `text-slate-500 dark:text-slate-400` — the dominant pattern, used for things like KPI trend labels. Verified: `#64748B` (slate-500) on white = **4.76:1, PASS**.
- `text-slate-400 dark:text-slate-500` — the *inverted* order, confirmed via `grep -c` at **162 occurrences across 31 files** (e.g. `AlarmDialog.jsx`, `CalculatorStepper.jsx`, `EmissionFactorsReference.jsx`, and others). Verified: `#94A3B8` (slate-400) on white = **2.56:1, FAIL** against both the 4.5:1 text threshold and the 3:1 non-text/icon threshold.

**Confirmed concrete case:** `src/components/alarms/AlarmDialog.jsx` renders on a `bg-white` panel (confirmed by reading the component) and uses `text-slate-400 dark:text-slate-500` for the Bell/MapPin/Clock meta icons (lines 111, 116, 135, 265, 288) and the "Sudah dibaca" label (line 274) — all at 2.56:1 in light mode, well below AA.

**Caveat:** I verified the specific `AlarmDialog.jsx` case directly against its real background. The other 161 occurrences across 30 more files very likely share the same problem (same class pairing, same design system, and the sampled instances - `CalculatorStepper.jsx`, `EmissionFactorsReference.jsx` - are also on light cards), but I did not individually confirm every single file's background before reporting this, since some could in principle sit inside an always-dark container where the inverted order would coincidentally be correct. Recommend fixing with per-file visual confirmation rather than a blind global find-and-replace.

**Fix:** swap the class order to the already-correct, already-dominant convention: `text-slate-400 dark:text-slate-500` → `text-slate-500 dark:text-slate-400`, checking each file's surrounding background as the swap is made (most card/panel backgrounds in this app are `bg-white`/`bg-slate-900`, matching the already-passing convention; skip any instance that's confirmed to sit inside a permanently dark element).

## Finding 2 — Weak focus indicator on 42 form inputs/selects, inconsistent with the rest of the app (MEDIUM)

**Rule:** R-32 (Keyboard Accessibility) — Hard Gate: a focus indicator must be visible.

**Where:** two different focus-style conventions are in active use:
- `focus:outline-none focus:ring-2 focus:ring-{color}-500` — a proper, clearly visible focus ring. Confirmed **13 instances** (mostly buttons and modal controls, e.g. `BaseModal.jsx`'s close button).
- `focus:outline-none focus:border-{color}-500` — removes the native outline and replaces it with *only* a 1px border-color shift. Confirmed **42 instances**, concentrated in filter dropdowns and search inputs across `Header.jsx`, `PenambahEmisiTab.jsx`, `PLTSTab.jsx`, `PLTSAnalyticsSection.jsx`, `TabSystemPerformancePr.jsx`, `EmissionFactorsReference.jsx`, `SearchableSelect.jsx`, and others.

**Why it's a finding, not just a style note:** R-32 requires a visible focus indicator on every interactive element, and the skill specifically names "a ring that only appears on hover" and thin indicators as the failure mode. A 1px border-color change is a real indicator (not literally invisible, so this is a step down from a true violation), but it's a materially weaker signal than the 2px ring with offset the app's own buttons and modals already use elsewhere, and 42 is a lot of inconsistency for one codebase - a keyboard user tabbing through a filter row would see a strong ring on the "Input Data" button next to it, then a faint border shift on the dropdown itself.

**Fix:** standardize the 42 inputs/selects on the same `focus:ring-2 focus:ring-{color}-500` pattern already used for buttons and modals in this app, for consistency and a stronger indicator.
